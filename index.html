The primer

How the instrument thinks

Start concrete, then abstract. Every term below is earned before it is used above.

1

Start with a molehill

A gardener sees a fresh mound of earth and reads it: moles. The mound does not look like a mole, and no one agreed that mounds mean moles. It signifies by brute connection, the way smoke means fire.

Three parts, always. The mound is the vehicle, the thing doing the signifying. The moles are the object, what it stands for. The gardener's understanding is the interpretant, what it grows in the mind.

2

The triad

A sign is not a thing but a three-place relation. Remove any corner and the rest goes silent: marks without an object are noise, an object with no interpretant is never read.

Vehicle (representamen): the marks themselves
Object: what the marks stand for
Interpretant: the understanding produced

The instrument keeps two refinements. The immediate object is what the text presents; the dynamic object is the real state of affairs, which the text may misdescribe. The immediate interpretant is the meaning the form allows; the dynamic is the reading actually produced; the final is what inquiry would converge on, with no latitude left.

3

Three questions

Ask three questions of any sign, in this order.

In itself, what is it? A mere quality (qualisign), a particular occurrence (sinsign), or a repeatable type (legisign). Most written text is the third: word forms you have seen a thousand times, this inscription their replica.

To its object, how does it point? By resemblance (icon), by brute connection (index), or by convention (symbol). Few signs are pure; the instrument names the dominant relation and lists the subordinates.

To its interpretant, what does it deliver? A qualitative possibility (rheme), an assertion about an actual fact (dicent, pronounced dye-sent), or a reasoned inference (delome).

4

Ten species, and semiosis

The admissibility rule: a sign's dependents cannot outrank it. Order each trichotomy by quality (1), brute fact (2), convention (3); the interpretant's level may not exceed the object's, and the object's may not exceed the vehicle's. Seventeen of twenty seven combinations fail this test.

What survives is ten species, from the rhematic iconic qualisign (a mere feeling of red) to the delomic symbolic legisign (a full proof). The instrument checks the rule and demotes, visibly, when a reading would break it.

Finally: every interpretant becomes a new sign. Reading never terminates; it chains. The instrument shows two links of the chain and leaves the rest as open questions, which is where inquiry begins.

Colophon

An honest instrument

Method

This instrument is heuristic, not an oracle. It reads surface markers: pronouns, names, tenses, similes, moods, inference words. It cannot know your context, and it says so in the plates.

Mixture is the norm. Almost every text does iconic, indexical, and symbolic work at once; the instrument names the dominant relation and lists the subordinates rather than forcing purity.

Peirce later sketched a 66-class extension. It was never finished, and scholars dispute it. This instrument implements the completed 1903 system of ten, plus the immediate and dynamic refinements.

Sources

The taxonomy follows the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Peirce's Theory of Signs: the sign triad, the three trichotomies, the ten admissible classes, and the semiosis chain.

The ten species and their glosses follow Peirce's 1903 Syllabus; the qualisign, sinsign, legisign, rheme, dicent, and delome (argument) definitions follow the same source.

The working method, staged dissection, exploded views, margin questions, and visible revision, follows the study habits of Leonardo's journals, without a drop of pastiche.

First compositions always err; every sound reading begins by correcting the page. Where this instrument misreads your words, amend the record: the ledger shows exactly which test failed.
