The primer

How the instrument thinks

Start concrete, then abstract. Every term below is earned before it is used above.

1

Start with a molehill

A gardener sees a fresh mound of earth and reads it: moles. The mound does not look like a mole, and no one agreed that mounds mean moles. It signifies by brute connection, the way smoke means fire.

Three parts, always. The mound is the sign (the representamen), the thing doing the signifying. The moles are the object, what it stands for. The gardener's understanding is the interpretant, what it grows in the mind.

2

The triad

Signification is irreducibly triadic: a sign is whatever functions in the three-place relation. Remove any corner and the rest goes silent: marks without an object are noise, an object with no interpretant is never read.

Sign (representamen): the marks themselves
Object: what the marks stand for
Interpretant: the understanding produced

The instrument keeps two refinements. The immediate object is the object as grasped at a stage of inquiry, possibly incomplete or wrong; the dynamic object is the object as it is, whatever kind of thing it is, which constrains the sign. The immediate interpretant is the meaning the form allows; the dynamic is the reading actually produced; the final is what inquiry would converge on, with no latitude left.

3

Three questions

Ask three questions of any sign, in this order.

In itself, what is it? A mere quality (qualisign), a particular occurrence (sinsign), or a repeatable type (legisign). Most written text is the third: word forms you have seen a thousand times, this inscription their replica.

To its object, how does it stand? By resemblance (icon), by brute connection (index), or by convention (symbol). Few signs are pure; the instrument names the dominant relation and lists the subordinates.

To its interpretant, what does it deliver? A term that asserts nothing (rheme), a sign represented as concerning actual existence, hence true or false (dicent, pronounced dye-sent), or an argument: premises working to a conclusion (delome).

4

Ten species, and semiosis

The admissibility rule: the later trichotomies cannot outrank the earlier. Order each trichotomy by quality (1), brute fact (2), convention (3); the interpretant's level may not exceed the object's, and the object's may not exceed the sign's. Seventeen of twenty seven combinations fail this test.

What survives is ten species, from the rhematic iconic qualisign (a mere feeling of red) to the delomic symbolic legisign (a full proof). When a reading would break the rule, the instrument flags the failure and asks for the earliest call to be re-examined, starting with the sign; it never demotes a reading to force a fit.

Finally, semiosis. In the early account, every interpretant becomes a new sign, and the chain has no fixed end. In the later account semiosis is end-directed: inquiry converges on a final interpretant, and there need be no further sign. The instrument shows two links of the early chain and leaves the rest as open questions, which is where inquiry begins.

Colophon

An honest instrument

Method

This instrument is heuristic, not an oracle. It reads surface markers: pronouns, names, tenses, similes, moods, inference words. It cannot know your context, and it says so on the bench.

Mixture is this instrument's working assumption, a design choice rather than Peirce's doctrine. Almost every text does iconic, indexical, and symbolic work at once; the instrument names the dominant relation and lists the subordinates rather than forcing purity.

Peirce later sketched a 66-class extension. It was never finished, and scholars dispute it. This instrument implements the completed 1903 system of ten, plus the immediate and dynamic refinements.

Sources

The taxonomy follows the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Peirce's Theory of Signs: the sign triad, the three trichotomies, the ten admissible classes, and the semiosis chain.

The ten species and their glosses follow Peirce's 1903 Syllabus; the qualisign, sinsign, legisign, rheme, dicent, and delome (argument) definitions follow the same source.

First compositions always err; every sound reading begins by correcting the page. Where this instrument misreads your words, amend the record: each triad corner carries its verdict and its test.
